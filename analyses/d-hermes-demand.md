# Shoals short-term lease demand — demand-side analysis

Analyst role: demand only. Ground truth: research data pack compiled 2026-09-30. No browsing. Market = Florence, Muscle Shoals, Sheffield, Tuscumbia (Lauderdale + Colbert).

Client context: Bobo Group / Bobo Family Group is preparing a proposal on short-term leases. Proposal specifics were not in the pack. This note underwrites **who rents, for how long, and where the shelf is empty**. It does not pick a building, a capital stack, or a city permit strategy.

Figures in brackets are pack sources. Math on those figures is labeled. Anything not in the pack is labeled **Assumption**.

---

## Executive summary

- The nightly vacation-rental market and the 30-day-plus furnished market are different products. AirDNA, AirROI, and Rabbu measure the first. They structurally miss the second, because Florence defines a short-term rental as a stay under 30 consecutive days [Ordinance].
- Nightly demand is a value leisure market, not a boom. Shoals Airbnb ADR is $168 vs $247 statewide — about 32% below Alabama [Rabbu]. Other reads put ADR at $149–$185 [AirDNA, AirROI]. Guests will not pay destination rates here.
- How full that leisure market is depends on which dataset you trust. Do not average them. High cluster: 52–55% occupancy and $25.2K–$27.5K per listing [AirDNA, guestfavorites]. Low cluster: 31–37.5% occupancy and about $19K per listing [Rabbu, AirROI]. Supply direction conflicts too (−12.7% listings vs +35% to +105%).
- Even the strong occupancy read does not show a sold-out event market. The only monthly occupancy series peaks at 48.7% [AirROI]. Music weekends and UNA athletics explain the May–August and October revenue peaks. They are mostly served. They are not the hole.
- The hole is the guest who cannot sign a normal 12-month unfurnished lease and should not be paying hotel rack for one to six months. Counted professional supply is a handful of units: four Sheffield corporate apartments, scattered furnished asks at $750–$1,200, plus Facebook and Furnished Finder posts. Branded extended-stay hotels within 50 miles: zero [WoodSpring, Choice].
- That guest clears the regulatory cliff on purpose. A stay of 30+ consecutive days is not a short-term rental in Florence, so it sits outside the permit, the 4-bedroom cap, and lodging tax [Ordinance]. Hotels are also outside the ordinance — the brand gap is a scale decision, not a legal ban.
- The open price band versus sampled hotels is real: 30 nights at Quality Inn is $2,790–$3,000; Comfort Suites is $4,170–$4,620 [Choice sample, Oct 2026]. Furnished asks sit at $750–$1,200. A mid-tier lease around $1,500–$2,000/month undercuts the hotel and still prices far above unfurnished rent (Muscle Shoals 1BR average about $806). Studio 6 exists as an unpriced weekly motel — so the gap is mid-tier reliability, not the cheapest bed.
- Highest-conviction unmet needs, in order: (1) contract clinicians near North Alabama Medical Center, (2) a Florence 1–2BR product leased at 30+ days rather than another 3–4BR Airbnb, (3) crew housing for the industrial pipeline, timed to construction, not to a press release. Insurance and relocation are real categories with almost no local counts. Do not underwrite them.
- What the gap is worth, on evidence: a small book, not a 100-key hotel. Defendable gross rent is about $120K–$300K a year for 8–15 units. A branded box at 75% occupancy would need on the order of $1.8–$2.3M of room revenue. Nothing in the pack shows that volume. The brands already passed.
- Do not read AirDNA’s 97/100 market score as a green light to add nightly supply. Rabbu’s ROI score is 50/100 and calls the supply/demand balance below average. The score conflict is the same occupancy conflict. The lease proposal wins by serving the stay length those scores do not measure.

## 1. How to read the demand numbers

Three providers, three universes. The pack says to reconcile them, not pick a favorite.

| | AirDNA | AirROI | Rabbu | guestfavorites |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Window | TTM Aug 2025–Aug 2026 | Aug 2025–Jul 2026 | Snapshot 2026-04-27 | Sep 2026 |
| What it counts | Airbnb + Vrbo + Booking, deduped | Airbnb-centric | Airbnb-listed only | Florence/Muscle Shoals Airbnbs |
| Active listings | 220 (−12.7% YoY) | 186 (supply +34.8%) | 94 (+105% YoY) | not given |
| Occupancy | 55% (+17.5% YoY) | 37.5% | 31% (state avg 38%) | 52% |
| ADR | $149 (−13.1% YoY) | $185 | $168 (state avg $247) | not given |
| RevPAR / RevPAN | $82 | $67 | $52 | not given |
| Revenue / listing | $27.5K (+64.7% YoY) | $18,962 | $19,102 ($1,591/mo) | $25,155 |
| Investor score | Market 97/100 (demand 92, seasonality 92, regulation 67, revenue growth 58, investability 97) | not scored; regulation “Low” | ROI 50/100, “Competitive Opportunity” | not scored |

**What agrees.** Entire homes dominate (98.4% [AirROI]). Stock is houses (72.6%), skewed to 2BR+3BR (63.5%), average sleep 5.1 [AirROI]. ADR is a tight band at $149–$185, all below Alabama’s $247 [Rabbu]. Annual revenue per listing, if you ignore the tails, sits between about $19K and $27.5K. There is a winter trough and a warm-season plus October revenue peak. This is a small-city leisure rental market with a value ceiling.

**What does not agree, and why it matters.**

- **Listings.** 94 vs 186 vs 220, and the year-over-year arrows point opposite ways. Rabbu’s +105% is an April Airbnb-only snapshot off a small base. AirDNA’s −12.7% is a later, deduped, multi-platform trailing year. These are not two measurements of one pile. Implied gross booking value of each universe, using that source’s own revenue per listing: about $1.80M (Rabbu: 94 × $19,102), $3.53M (AirROI: 186 × $18,962), $6.05M (AirDNA: 220 × $27,500). Quote the range. Do not quote a blended “the Shoals STR market is $X.”
- **Occupancy.** 31% to 55%. Booked-night scale of each universe, occupancy times 365 times listings: about 10,600 (Rabbu), 25,500 (AirROI), 44,200 (AirDNA). Definitions differ (booked nights vs available nights), so even these are ranges inside ranges. AirDNA’s “+17.5% YoY” is ambiguous: if relative, prior-year occupancy was about 47%; if it meant 17.5 points, prior year was 37.5%, which would match today’s AirROI figure. The pack does not resolve that.
- **Internal checks, so later revenue claims stay honest.** AirDNA RevPAR $82 is consistent with ADR $149 at 55% occupancy (0.55 × $149 = $82). Stated annual revenue $27.5K is a bit under RevPAR × 365 ($29,930), which fits some blocked nights. AirROI’s stated $18,962 is well under both RevPAR × 365 ($24,455) and occupancy × ADR × 365 ($25,322) — use the stated annual figure, not a reconstructed one. Rabbu 1BR–3BR stated revenue is in the neighborhood of ADR × occupancy × 365. Rabbu 4BR is not: $280 × 17% × 365 ≈ $17,400, but stated annual revenue is $31,205. Treat 4BR **occupancy of 17%** as the demand fact. Treat 4BR **dollar revenue as unreliable**.
- **Seasonality score.** AirDNA’s seasonality component is 92/100. The pack does not define the scale. Higher-is-better is the usual AirDNA convention, which would mean seasonality is not a disqualifier versus other U.S. markets. It does not mean the market is flat. Rabbu’s own months show June revenue at 2.7× January [Rabbu].

**The category error to avoid.** A 31% Airbnb occupancy does not mean “the Shoals has no housing demand.” It means nightly leisure listings are not full. Stays of 30+ days are outside Florence’s STR definition, and the tools above are STR tools. The extended-stay gap is invisible to the score that looks best (AirDNA 97) and to the score that looks worst (Rabbu 50). Both are scoring the other product.

**Confidence on the leisure market:** HIGH that it is a below-state-ADR entire-home market with a winter trough. LOW on a single occupancy rate. Use 31–55% as the band [Rabbu, AirDNA].

### Where demand sits, by city

Unmet need is not spread evenly across “the Shoals.”

- **Florence is the concentration.** NAMC is there. UNA is there. The industrial park the pack names is Florence-Lauderdale. Downtown, North Florence, South Florence, Wilson Dam, Cypress Landing, Kona, and Elgie’s Mill are the neighborhoods the rental datasets bother to name [AirROI]. Florence is also the only city in the pack with an STR ordinance, which is what makes a 30+ day lease a distinct product rather than a gray area.
- **Muscle Shoals is second, and mostly for a different guest.** Studios (FAME, Muscle Shoals Sound) pull weekend tourists — served by the existing Airbnb stock. Workforce evidence is real but thinner: the nurse-group example listing is on River Road, and furnished asks show up at Stonebrook ($750 1BR / $975 2BR) and Alpine Village “Corporate” (listed, unpriced). No STR ordinance found — verify before treating that as a free pass [Ordinance note].
- **Sheffield is the supply comp, not the demand center.** Four corporate apartments on forrent.com are the only counted professional cluster in the pack. That is the embryonic extended-stay shelf. It is also the evidence the shelf is tiny.
- **Tuscumbia is last for this proposal.** Music Hall of Fame and a Comfort Inn at $112–$124/night [Choice]. No ordinance found. No hospital, no industrial spec building, no nurse channel called out. Fine for event overflow. Not where unmet monthly demand concentrates.
- **Lake and waterfront are the wrong map.** Lake access is 16% of Airbnb listings and waterfront 11% [Rabbu]. That is leisure spice, not where a clinician or a crew needs to sleep.

### What each segment will actually pay

Two ceilings, two buyers. Mixing them up is how a proposal prices itself out.

| Anchor | Monthly equivalent | Who it binds |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle Shoals unfurnished 1BR average ~$806 (range $450–$1,300) | ~$806 | A local who can sign a normal lease. Not the target. |
| Stonebrook furnished 1BR / 2BR | $750 / $975 | Price-sensitive furnished ask, if a unit is actually free. |
| Florence furnished example, 2710 Milford St | $1,200 | The only Florence furnished ask in the pack. About 1.5× the unfurnished average. |
| Quality Inn Florence East, Oct 2026 nightly × 30 | $2,790–$3,000 | Budget traditional hotel, no weekly discount observed in the pack. |
| Comfort Inn Tuscumbia × 30 | $3,360–$3,720 | Mid hotel. |
| Comfort Suites Florence × 30 | $4,170–$4,620 | Upper sampled hotel. Marriott Shoals is the unnamed premium anchor; not priced here. |
| STR rack × 30 at pack ADRs, before any monthly discount | $4,470–$5,550 | $149–$185 × 30. A discount large enough to beat Quality Inn has to be deep (see §3). |

A $1,800 furnished month is about 2.2× unfurnished average rent ($1,800 / $806). A local tenant will not pay that. The buyer has to be someone whose alternative is a hotel night, a Facebook post, or not taking the assignment. That is the segment definition: **temporary by the job, not by preference.**

**Assumption (not in the pack):** staffing agencies often hold the housing stipend, so the economic buyer may be the agency, not the nurse. A proposal that cannot invoice a firm is aimed at the weaker payer. Confidence MEDIUM on that industry pattern, NONE from local evidence.

## 3. Length-of-stay demand vs the 30-day cliff

Florence Ordinance 2023-22 (adopted 7/10/2023): a short-term rental is a rental for fewer than 30 consecutive days. Stays of 30+ consecutive days are not STRs. They are long-term residential rentals. The ordinance also does not apply to hotels, motels, inns, and B&Bs [Ordinance]. Lodging tax applies to stays of 30 days or less; the pack says a monthly furnished lease avoids STR permitting, the occupancy cap, and TOT exposure. The tax rate itself is not in the pack, so the dollar savings from skipping TOT are real in direction and unquantified in size.

Sheffield, Tuscumbia, Muscle Shoals, and unincorporated county: no STR ordinance found in public sources as of 2026-09-30. That is “not found,” not “proven none.” Verify before operating [Ordinance note].

### Who naturally sits on each side

| Stay length | Who | What they need the product to be | Regulatory bucket in Florence |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–4 nights | Music tourists, UNA game weekends, theatre, faire | A house, a weekend, self check-in | STR. Permit, 4BR cap, no parties, local contact on 30-minute response, TOT. |
| 5–29 nights | A longer family visit, a short insurance stay, someone gaming the line | Nightly or weekly, still a lodging product | Still an STR. Still taxed. A 29-night stay at pack ADR is $4,321–$5,365 before tax — worse than Quality Inn’s 30-night rack ($2,790–$3,000) unless the host discounts hard. |
| 30–90 days | Nurse early in a contract, house-hunting relocate, longer displacement | Furnished, utilities clear, kitchen, washer, parking, fast move-in | Not an STR if it is one consecutive lease. This is the empty professional shelf. |
| 91 days (13 weeks) to 6 months | Full nurse assignment, small crew, some insurance | Same, plus a payer who will sign and can be invoiced | Same. Deepest fit for a lease model. |
| 3–12 months | Industrial crews once a site is active | Cluster of 1BRs or a few plain houses, durable furnishing, not decor | Same. Highest nights per guest. Lowest evidence it is happening now. |

### The cliff is a product line, not a footnote

Guests do not experience “Ordinance 2023-22.” They experience a shelf that changes at day 30.

- **Under 30 days, the shelf is crowded and the wrong shape.** 98.4% entire homes, houses 72.6%, 3BR is 32.3% of listings, average sleep 5.1 [AirROI]. Modal listing is a family vacation house. Booking lead time is 41 days [AirROI] — a leisure planning cycle. Only 37.6% of listings show an exact location [AirROI], which is a real defect for someone who needs to know the drive to a hospital shift. Workspace is in 53% of listings and pets in 39% [Rabbu]. Kitchen (95%), parking (93%), washer (82%), and self check-in (87%) are already common [Rabbu]. The hardware for a monthly guest is mostly there. The term, the size, and the reliability are not.
- **At 30 days and over, the shelf is a scrap pile.** Stonebrook at $750/$975, one Florence 1BR ask at $1,200, four Sheffield corporate apartments, an unpriced Alpine Village corporate listing, a corporatehousing.com aggregator page, Airbnb monthly-stay and VRBO long-stay pages, Furnished Finder, and Facebook. That is a channel list, not a product. Zero WoodSpring / MainStay / Everhome / Suburban within the Choice extended-stay filter for Florence [Choice]. Zero WoodSpring within 50 miles [WoodSpring].
- **Splitting one Florence unit across the line is how operators get regulated by accident.** A single sub-30-day stay can pull the unit into the STR definition. The workforce book and the weekend book should not be the same listing. Cities with no ordinance found are more flexible on paper and less proven on demand. Demand concentration still points at Florence.

### Discount math: nightly STR does not become extended-stay by adding a monthly coupon

Pack ADRs times 30 nights, no discount: $4,470 (AirDNA $149), $5,040 (Rabbu $168), $5,550 (AirROI $185).

**Assumption, not in the pack:** platforms often show 20–40% off for monthly stays. Even a 40% discount leaves $2,682–$3,330 for 30 nights. That only barely kisses Quality Inn’s $2,790–$3,000, and it still may be a stay under 30 days — permit, tax, and STR rules attached. A true 30+ day residential lease is a different contract (Alabama landlord-tenant rules instead of a guest agreement). Whether a host can chain 29-day bookings and stay outside the ordinance is a legal question, not a demand question. Do not build the model on that trick. Confidence HIGH that the clean demand is a real 30+ day lease. Confidence NONE on chained-booking workarounds — out of scope.

### Length mix implied by the rental stock

Rabbu by size is the best stay-length tell in the pack, with the 4BR revenue caveat from §1:

| Size | ADR | Occupancy | Stated revenue / yr | What the occupancy says |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1BR | $132 | 29% | $15,549 | Closest thing to an extended-stay unit. Weak as a nightly product. |
| 2BR | $114 | 35% | $16,981 | Best fill, worst rate. The size a couple or a small crew might want, sold as a cheap vacation. |
| 3BR | $171 | 32% | $20,469 | Workhorse leisure listing. Best stated RevPAN at $55. |
| 4BR | $280 | 17% | $31,205 (does not reconcile with ADR × occ × 365) | Event house. Dead most of the year. About 111 booked nights if you divide stated revenue by ADR — still a weekend product, and the two Rabbu 4BR figures cannot both be precise. |

The market is already telling size. Big houses fill like event inventory. Small units fill a bit better and earn less per night, because they are priced as leftover vacation product instead of as housing. That is the mismatch a 30+ day book is for.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the legal line (ordinance text is in the pack). HIGH on which segments fall on which side, given the 13-week assumption for nurses. MEDIUM on the dollar width of the corridor, because Studio 6’s weekly rate is missing and October hotel rates may not be the winter rate.

## 4. Why zero branded extended-stay hotels exist, and what that gap is worth

### Facts

- WoodSpring Suites: no location within 50 miles of Florence. Alabama sites named in the pack: Huntsville, Madison, Prattville only [WoodSpring].
- Choice extended-stay filter (WoodSpring, MainStay, Everhome, Suburban): “0 Extended Stay hotels near Florence, AL,” checked 2026-09-30 [Choice].
- Weekly and monthly budget stays therefore sit with independents (Studio 6 Florence is on TripAdvisor’s weekly-rates list; rate not in the pack) and with OTA monthly listings [pack §2].
- Sampled traditional hotels, October 2026: Quality Inn $93–$100, Comfort Suites $139–$154, Comfort Inn Tuscumbia $112–$124 [Choice]. Marriott Shoals Hotel & Spa is the upscale anchor, unpriced.

### Why the brands are absent

This is inference from the facts above, not a quote from a brand’s site. Confidence tags on each reason.

1. **It is a density pass, not a ban. Confidence HIGH.** Hotels and motels are exempt from Ordinance 2023-22 [Ordinance]. A WoodSpring in Florence would be legal as a hotel. The 50-mile void, with sites in Huntsville and Madison and none here, is the footprint of a chain that already placed its Alabama capex in the larger market. Florence did not clear the hurdle.
2. **Demand is real and too split to pre-lease a 100-key box. Confidence MEDIUM.** Four cities. One regional hospital. Festival demand that is short-stay and, on AirROI’s peak month, still under 50% occupied. Industrial demand the pack itself puts in the future tense. Brands underwrite on contracted corporate nights. The pack shows channels (Furnished Finder, Facebook, OTA monthly, four corporate apartments). It does not show a master account.
3. **Entrepreneur capital went into Airbnb houses, which are the wrong building for this guest. Confidence MEDIUM.** Rabbu listings +105% YoY; AirROI supply +34.8%. Whatever the AirDNA listing decline says about the later year, the recent story on the Airbnb-centric reads is “more vacation houses,” not “an extended-stay hotel.” Those houses sleep 5.1 and live under the 30-day line. They do not fill the brand gap. They widen the product mismatch.
4. **A new hotel needs occupancy the leisure data does not show. Confidence MEDIUM.** Extended-stay brands are built for something like 70%+ occupancy and a weekly rate, with no restaurant and a small staff (**Assumption** on the brand model, not a pack figure). Shoals STR occupancy is 31–55%, and ADR is a leisure rate ($149–$185), not a proven weekly corporate rate. Paying hotel construction costs against that curve is how a brand says no. An existing 1–2BR on a 30+ day lease does not have that cost curve. That is why a family group can look at a gap a REIT walked past.
5. **Studio 6 may already be “good enough” at the bottom. Confidence LOW-MEDIUM — the rate is missing.** The pack’s own implication is that budget weekly demand uses independents and OTAs. If that motel is clean enough and cheap enough, the leftover gap is not “any bed.” It is a mid-tier bed: kitchen, washer, a lease, an invoice, a known location near NAMC, pet and desk possible. Do not model this as a discount to Studio 6. Model it as a discount to Quality Inn and Comfort, at a quality step up from a weekly motel and a Facebook sublet.

### What the gap is worth

Three numbers. Only one of them is a planning figure.

**A. Guest-level spread versus hotels in the pack — evidenced arithmetic, not a forecast.**

A 13-week assignment is 91 nights (**Assumption** on length). Hotel cost if they pay the October rack every night:

| Alternative | Cost for 91 nights | Furnished lease at $1,800 × 3 months | Guest (or agency) saves |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quality Inn at $93 | $8,463 | $5,400 | $3,063 |
| Quality Inn at $100 | $9,100 | $5,400 | $3,700 |
| Comfort Suites at $139 | $12,649 | $5,400 | $7,249 |

At $1,500 × 3 months ($4,500), savings versus Quality Inn $93 are about $4,000. At $2,200 × 3 ($6,600), savings versus Quality Inn $93 shrink to about $1,900, and versus Comfort Suites they are still about $6,000. **The ceiling that matters is the cheap traditional hotel, not the nice one.** A rate much above $2,000/month stops being an easy yes for anyone who would have tolerated Quality Inn. Comfort Suites guests can pay more; do not assume that is the typical workforce buyer.

Per completed nurse assignment, operator rent at $1,500–$2,000 is $4,500–$6,000. That is the unit of demand. A book is that number times how many assignments you can actually house.

**B. Portfolio gross — the defendable worth of the gap. Scenarios, not facts.**

Unit counts are **Assumptions**. The only counted professional stock in the pack is four Sheffield corporate apartments plus scattered single asks. The low case is “a bit more than what already exists.” The base case is “nurses are a repeating book and the corridor is real.” The high case requires the industrial pipeline to actually hire bodies.

| Case | Belief required | Units | Rate / mo | Months filled | Annual gross rent | Confidence |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Low | Market absorbs a little more than today’s counted corporate stock; some vacancy; price near the top of scattered asks | 8 | $1,500 | 10 | $120,000 | MEDIUM that this floor is reachable if execution is decent |
| Base | Repeating clinician demand, Florence 30+ day lease, rate inside the hotel-undercut band | 12–15 | $1,800 | 11 | $238,000–$297,000 | LOW-MEDIUM on the unit count; MEDIUM on the rate |
| High | Base, plus either a fatter nurse book or one project placing extra people for half a year | 25–30 | $2,000 | 11 | $550,000–$660,000 | LOW |

Scale check, so this does not get confused with “capturing the Airbnb market.” A 15-unit book filled 11 months is on the order of 5,000 occupied nights a year (15 × 11 × 30). That is about 11% of AirDNA’s implied booked nights (~44,200) and almost half of Rabbu’s (~10,600). It is a niche beside the multi-platform STR market. It is not a bet that leisure demand doubles. And those STR night counts do not include today’s 30+ day leases — so the comparison is a ceiling check, not a share-of-market claim.

A single industrial project, separately: 15 people × $1,800 × 6 months ≈ $162,000 of project rent; 20 × $1,600 × 6 months = $192,000. Useful upside. Not a base-case annuity, because the pack says “future.”

**C. The hotel-shaped number — illustration of what the brands would need, not an estimate of Shoals demand.**

An 80-key box at 75% occupancy and $80 ADR is about 21,900 room-nights and about $1.75M revenue. A 100-key box at 75% and $85 ADR is about 27,400 room-nights and about $2.3M. **Assumption on keys, occupancy, and ADR** — none of those are Shoals extended-stay facts, because the product does not exist here. Nothing in the pack (no bed count, no crew count, no corporate room-night contract) supports that revenue. Using it as “what the gap is worth” would invent a market the brands already declined to build.

**Worth, stated plainly.** The gap is worth a small mid-tier furnished book in the $120K–$300K gross-rent range if clinician demand is even modestly real. It is not worth a new flagged hotel on this evidence. It is also not worth a race to $750 against Stonebrook. The capturable guest is the one now headed for a $93–$154 hotel night or a Facebook sublet, paying roughly $1,500–$2,000 for a 30+ day lease that a staffing firm can invoice.

**Confidence on worth:** HIGH that the brand shelf is empty. HIGH that hotel rack × 30 sits above furnished asks. MEDIUM that a small book can clear $120K–$300K. LOW that demand supports more than about 15 stabilized units before an industrial project starts. UNKNOWN versus Studio 6 until that rate is collected.

| Pack synthesis | Revenue peaks May–August + October | Trough January–February | Use this as the agreed shape. Do not erase the March/April disagreement. |

**Reconcile March.** AirROI calls March a peak and April a low month. Rabbu’s highlighted months jump to strength in May and do not list March. So either AirROI’s March peak is real and Rabbu’s snapshot under-weights it, or it is a one-year quirk (a tournament, a weather window, a data artifact). Do not build a March thesis on one provider. The agreed dead zone is January–February. April is dead in AirROI and merely “before the May ramp” in Rabbu. Treat April as soft.

**Also: the peak is not a sellout.** 48.7% occupancy in AirROI’s best month means August still has half the listed nights empty in that dataset. Festival and football demand raise revenue. They do not, on this evidence, max out the city. Weekend-versus-weekday splits are not in the pack. A 4BR at 17% annual occupancy [Rabbu] is consistent with full Saturdays and empty Tuesdays. That is a yield problem for big houses, not proof the Shoals needs more event inventory.

### What this means for a lease book

- **Music and UNA are the seasonal STR. They are not the filler.** They pile into May–August and October, on 1–4 night stays, in 3–4BR houses. A proposal that buys that calendar is buying the product Rabbu already scores as a below-average supply/demand balance, with a June-to-January revenue swing of 2.7×.
- **Clinicians are the only current segment that should occupy January, February, and April.** Hospital staffing does not follow the Handy festival. That counter-seasonality is logic plus the existence of year-round nurse channels, not a monthly census. Confidence MEDIUM. It is still the best explanation available for who would pay rent in the months the STR series calls dead.
- **Crews are not seasonal. They are binary.** Zero until a shovel is in the ground, then continuous for 3–12 months, including the trough. That is the best occupancy shape in the map and the least evidenced current demand. Do not staff a portfolio for crews who are not hired yet. Do keep the lease form flexible enough to take them when the spec building or the Advantage Site actually starts.
- **Insurance is a spike, and the spike lands on the wrong side of the cliff more often than not.** A storm week fills hotels and Airbnbs for days to a few weeks. It does not, by itself, justify 30+ day inventory. Longer displacements exist. The pack has no local count. Leave it as surge optionality on units you already filled with clinicians.
- **Operational consequence of the cliff.** The segment that fills the trough (30+ day clinicians and crews) and the segment that fills October weekends (STR) should be different units if they sit in Florence. One listing cannot legally be both without falling under the ordinance the moment it takes a short stay.

**Confidence:** HIGH on the agreed May–August + October peak and the January–February trough. MEDIUM on assigning those peaks to UNA plus music rather than to some unnamed driver. MEDIUM on clinician counter-seasonality. LOW on March. LOW on insurance as a calendar item.

## 6. The three highest-conviction unmet needs

Ranked by evidence the buyer exists × evidence the shelf is empty × stay length on the lease side of the cliff × a price band wide enough to matter. Not ranked by fantasy TAM.

Music tourism and UNA game weekends are **met needs**. They are why 94–220 listings exist. Peak-month occupancy still under 50% in the only monthly series [AirROI], 4BR occupancy 17% [Rabbu], and Airbnb-centric supply growth of +35% to +105% are the fingerprints of a served, competitive short-stay market. Building another event house is the crowded trade. It is not on this list.

### 1. Contract clinician housing near NAMC — highest conviction

**The need.** A furnished 1–2BR, 30 to 90+ days, short drive to North Alabama Medical Center, bookable inside a couple of weeks, with a kitchen, washer, parking, a desk, and a stated pet policy. Invoiceable to a staffing firm if the stipend sits with the agency (**Assumption**).

**Why this ranks first.** It is the only segment with active local search channels named in the pack: Furnished Finder serving Florence, and Travel Nurse Housing USA posts for Shoals addresses. NAMC is named as the driver. The stay (about 91 days, assumption) is unambiguously on the lease side of Ordinance 2023-22. The alternative in the pack is a hotel at $2,790–$4,620 for 30 nights or a scattered ask at $750–$1,200 that may not be free, furnished to a professional standard, or near the hospital. Workspace at 53% and pets at 39% of the Airbnb stock [Rabbu] show the vacation inventory was not built for this guest even before the 30-day line.

**Where.** Florence first. Muscle Shoals only as overflow (the River Road example shows some supply already advertised there). Not the lake. Not a 4BR downtown party-limited STR.

**Size.** Do not print a fake census. Planning range: a handful to a few dozen concurrent people. Ten concurrent assignments is a real book ($4,500–$6,000 rent per assignment at the rates in §4, several turns per year per unit). Twenty-five concurrent is the top of what this evidence can carry without a bed count. Confidence on existence MEDIUM-HIGH. Confidence on “more than 15 stabilized units from nurses alone” LOW.

**What would kill the thesis.** Furnished Finder listings sitting vacant at $1,200 with no takers, or Studio 6 (once priced) turning out to be clean, pet-friendly, and already contracted to the agencies. Those checks are not in the pack. They are the next facts to get. Until then, the search channels are enough to rank this first, not enough to buy 30 units.

### 2. A Florence 1–2BR leased at 30+ days — the structural hole

**The need.** Not a new customer so much as the missing shelf the first customer (and the third) have to stand on. Professional monthly furnished housing inside Florence: smaller than the modal Airbnb, longer than a guest agreement, priced between $1,500 and $2,000, never dipped under 30 days if it is to stay outside the ordinance.

**Why this ranks second, ahead of crews.** The empty shelf is better evidenced than any single future buyer. Zero branded extended-stay within 50 miles [WoodSpring, Choice]. Counted corporate supply is four Sheffield apartments plus one-off asks. STR stock is the wrong size (sleeps 5.1, 3BR-heavy) and the wrong term (under 30 days, 41-day lead time). Rabbu’s 2BR has the best occupancy (35%) and the worst ADR ($114) — the market’s closest size to this need, mispriced as a cheap vacation and still not a lease. Florence is where the ordinance draws the line that makes this product legally distinct, and Florence is where the hospital and the named industrial park are.

**Where it concentrates.** Florence city, ordinary neighborhoods with a short drive to NAMC and, secondarily, toward Florence-Lauderdale Industrial Park. Not Wilson Dam waterfront. Not a Sweetwater weekend house. Sheffield’s four corporate units are the comp to beat, not the place to pile more of the same unless Florence sites cannot be had.

**Size.** This is the same 8–15 unit base case as §4 ($120K–$297K gross depending on rate and fill). It is unmet need in the sense that search is informal and branded supply is zero. It is not unmet need in the sense of a waiting list the pack counted. Confidence HIGH that the shelf is empty. MEDIUM that 8–15 units lease up at $1,500–$2,000. LOW above that, until nurse counts or signed crew contracts exist.

**What not to build in this slot.** A 4BR, a lake house, or a nightly STR that “also offers monthly.” In Florence those are different businesses, and the nightly one is the crowded one.

### 3. Project-length crew housing on the industrial corridor — highest nights per guest, weakest current proof

**The need.** Plain furnished housing, 3–12 months, for trades on the Florence Advantage Site and the 150,000 sq ft spec building in Florence-Lauderdale Industrial Park, when those projects are actually under construction [pack §4]. Clustered 1BRs or simple multi-bedroom houses. Corporate lease. Not decor, not a hot tub (hot tubs are 7% of Airbnb listings [Rabbu] — leisure residue).

**Why it ranks third, not first.** The pack is explicit that future crews need this, and the stay length is the best economic fit in the whole map (continuous occupancy, trough included, far past the cliff). It ranks below the first two because the verb is “future.” Statewide $14.6B and 9,388 jobs are not Shoals demand. No ground-breaking date, no general contractor, no crew size. Underwriting units today against a marketed site is how a family group owns vacant furniture.

**Where.** Florence-Lauderdale industrial side, not downtown and not Tuscumbia. Muscle Shoals and Sheffield only if the commute is obviously shorter to the specific site — the pack does not locate the Advantage Site beyond the EDA’s Shoals marketing, so do not pretend a pin on the map.

**Size.** One real project could be worth on the order of $160K–$190K of rent over six months at the rates in §4, for 15–20 people (**Assumption** on headcount). That is a call option on the same unit type as need 2, not a reason to double the portfolio in advance. Confidence MEDIUM that the need appears if and when construction starts. LOW on when. NONE on current occupancy from this segment.

**How to hold the option.** Lease form and furnishing package that a crew boss can sign for 90 days and extend. Do not hold units dark waiting. Fill them with need 1 until a project date is real.

### Explicitly not in the top three

- **Insurance displacement.** No local counts. Mostly under 30 days when it hits. Surge on a book you already have, not a segment to build for. Confidence LOW.
- **Corporate relocation / new-plant employees.** Same pipeline as crews, lagged, and many of those hires want a normal unfurnished lease near $806, not a $1,800 furnished month. The furnished window is the house-hunting gap only. Confidence LOW.
- **More music-weekend or football-weekend inventory.** Served, price-capped, and not full even in peak month on the AirROI series. The top 10% of AirROI listings already clear $3,996+/month at 75%+ occupancy and $319+ ADR — quality nightly product works for someone. That someone is not the unmet guest in this note.
- **A WoodSpring-shaped hotel.** The gap is real and too small, on this evidence, for the building type that would close it in a brand’s model. See §4.C.

## TL;DR

1. Nightly Shoals demand is value leisure — ADR $149–$185, well under Alabama’s $247 — and it is already housed in entire-home Airbnbs that are not full, even in peak month (48.7% in the only monthly occupancy series).
2. The datasets disagree on occupancy (31–55%) and on whether supply is growing or shrinking; quote the range, and do not treat any of them as a measure of 30-day-plus demand, which Florence law puts outside the STR category.
3. Unmet need concentrates in Florence, in furnished 1–2BR stays of 30 days to about six months, for people who cannot sign a normal lease and should not be paying $2,790–$4,620 a month in a traditional hotel.
4. Zero branded extended-stay hotels within 50 miles is a scale pass, not a legal ban; the gap is worth roughly $120K–$300K a year of gross rent on 8–15 units, not a new 100-key hotel.
5. Underwrite clinicians first, keep the same unit type ready for industrial crews when a project actually starts, and do not buy another 4BR weekend house or an insurance-spike story the pack cannot count.
