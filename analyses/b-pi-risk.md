# B — Contrarian Risk Analysis: Shoals Short-Term-Lease Entry

**Analyst:** Pi (designated skeptic) · **Date:** 2026-09-30 · **Ground truth:** `research/shoals-stl-data-pack.md` (no browsing)
**Mandate:** Stress-test the bull case. Where providers conflict, I take the side the bull case doesn't want.

---

## Executive summary (read this if nothing else)

1. **The three data providers do not describe the same market.** Occupancy is 31% [Rabbu], 37.5% [AirROI], or 55% [AirDNA]; revenue is ~$19K [Rabbu], ~$19K [AirROI], or $27.5K [AirDNA]. Two of three cluster low; AirDNA is the outlier the bull case leans on.
2. **AirDNA's headline (+64.7% revenue/listing, −12.7% listings) is a survivorship signature, not an opportunity.** Weak inventory exited; the remaining cohort's averages rose mechanically. A new entrant does not inherit the survivors' averages — they inherit the median.
3. **Supply is growing in exactly the channel a new entrant must compete in:** Airbnb listings +105% YoY [Rabbu], supply +34.8% [AirROI]. That is a shakeout, and entering a shakeout means buying someone else's exit.
4. **Price is already falling while occupancy "rises":** ADR −13.1% YoY [AirDNA] — occupancy bought with discounts, with budget hotels ($93–124/night [Data §2]) capping pricing power from below.
5. **Half the market is unprofitable-tier:** bottom 25% runs ~20% occupancy, $967/mo [AirROI]; median is ~38% / $1,802/mo. A day-one entrant should expect 2nd–4th quartile performance, not top-decile.
6. **The market has likely crossed the STR/LTR crossover:** base-case nightly STR nets ~$7–8K/yr on a ~$300K asset; the same house long-term-rented nets ~$11–13K with near-zero volatility. Median nightly STR is now the *worse* version of a simpler business.
7. **Leveraged nightly STR is cash-negative in every scenario** at current asset prices (illustration: −$5K to −$15K/yr after P&I; §4).
8. **Demand is real but thinner and more seasonal than the bull story:** healthcare contracts are the only durable driver; university demand is a handful of weekends; music heritage is a day-trip attraction set; construction-crew demand is a marketing brochure, not a tenant.
9. **Regulation is manageable today but directionally adverse:** Florence already passed STR law (2023); three neighboring cities show a regulatory vacuum that "not found" ≠ "none" [Data §3], and vacuums close.
10. **The steelman that survived my attack is not nightly STR at all** — it's the furnished 30+ day lease (mid-term), which the Florence ordinance explicitly exempts from STR rules, monetizes the most durable demand driver, and floors out at the ordinary rental market.

## 2. Oversupply risk — reconciling 31% vs 55% occupancy

### 2.1 The supply counts themselves conflict

| Provider | Listings | YoY supply change | Channel | Window |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| AirDNA | 220 | **−12.7%** | Airbnb+Vrbo+Booking dedup | TTM to Aug 2026 |
| AirROI | 186 | **+34.8%** | Airbnb-centric | Aug 2025–Jul 2026 |
| Rabbu | 94 | **+105%** | Airbnb-only, listed units | Apr 2026 snapshot |

All three can be true simultaneously, and that synthesis is itself the finding: **new hosts are flooding into the Airbnb channel (+105%, +34.8%) while multi-platform/legacy inventory is exiting (−12.7% dedup count).** That is a market in churn — new entrants in, failed operators out — i.e., a shakeout, not a growth market. Entering during the shakeout means competing against desperate new supply at the same time you inherit their pricing pressure.

### 2.2 What 31% vs 55% each implies

| | If 31% is true [Rabbu] | If 55% is true [AirDNA] |
|---|---|---|
| Utilization | Units sit empty ~69% of nights; RevPAN $52 | ~200 booked nights/yr; RevPAR $82 |
| Typical revenue | ~$19.1K/yr gross [Rabbu]; avg home value $349K → ~5.5% *gross* yield | $27.5K/yr gross [AirDNA]; implies a professionalized, ~full-time-available cohort (0.55 × $149 × 365 = $29.9K at full availability vs $27.5K reported → ~92% availability — day-one entrants don't get that) |
| Market structure | Bottom quartile (~20% occ, $967/mo [AirROI]) is dying; consolidation underway | Weak hosts already left; survivors scaled; market "reformed" |
| Entry implication | You are buying into the exit wave | You can win with top-quartile product + operations |

**Reconciliation (my read, medium-high confidence):** both are partially true, and that partial truth is adverse to the bull case. Rabbu and AirROI — two independent providers — agree on the entrant-relevant numbers (occ 31–37.5%, revenue ~$19K, supply growing). AirDNA is the outlier, and its own internals carry the tell: revenue/listing **+64.7%** while listings **−12.7%** and ADR **−13.1%**. Removing low-occupancy inventory mechanically raises survivor averages (survivorship bias), and "occupancy up while price falls" means occupancy was *bought with discounts* — margin compression dressed as momentum. AirDNA's Market Score 97/100 is a screening heuristic, not a pro forma.

**Decision rule:** underwrite to what a *new, median* listing experiences — the Rabbu/AirROI cluster (occ 31–38%, gross $18–21K) — and treat AirDNA's world as the ceiling achievable only with top-decile execution.

### 2.3 Quantified: the STR/LTR crossover has likely been crossed

Assumptions (mine, stated): 3BR house bought ~$300K (below the $349K average [Rabbu]); opex lines per §4; local unfurnished 3BR rent $1,300–1,600/mo (interpolated from 1BR avg ~$806 and $450–$1,300 range [Data §2]).

| Model | Gross/yr | Opex/yr | Net/yr | Unlevered yield on $300K |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Nightly STR, base case (35% occ, $160 ADR) | ~$20.4K | ~$12.5K | **~$7.9K** | ~2.6% |
| Nightly STR, top-quartile (58% occ, $210 ADR [AirROI tier]) | ~$44.5K | ~$15K | ~$29K | ~9.7% *(requires beating 75% of incumbents on day one)* |

**Conclusion:** the median nightly-STR operator now nets *less than a boring landlord* on the same asset, with more volatility, labor, and regulatory exposure. Even the AirROI top-25% tier ($2,824/mo gross = ~$33.9K/yr) nets roughly LTR-parity after STR opex. Only the top ~10% clearly out-earns LTR — and the pack gives no reason to believe a new entrant lands there: only 11% of listings have waterfront and 7% hot tubs [Rabbu], i.e., the premium amenity moats are already occupied.

## 3. Regulatory risk

**Florence (Ordinance 2023-22, Ch. 5 Art. IX) — rated: Medium today, directionally adverse.**
- Compliance stack for nightly STR: permit + certificate of occupancy + business license, annual renewal, Fire Marshal inspection, STR insurance, tax-compliance proof, and a Local Contact Person who must respond to city notice within **30 minutes** [Data §3]. Modest fees, real operational teeth — professional operators absorb this; casual hosts exit (consistent with the shakeout in §2.1).
- The trend matters more than the text: the city proved in 2023 it will regulate. Permit → cap → restriction is the standard escalation path in tourist-adjacent towns as complaints accumulate. A 3-year-old ordinance with no amendment track record in the pack = unpriced tail risk of tightening (e.g., R-1 bans, numeric caps).
- TOT/lodging tax applies to stays ≤30 days, and platform remittance is *unverified* ("may remit — verify with AL DOR") [Data §3] — mis-remission liability lands on the operator.

**Sheffield, Tuscumbia, Muscle Shoals, unincorporated county — rated: unpriced vacuum risk.**
"No ordinance found in public sources as of 2026-09-30" [Data §3] is not "no ordinance exists" — the pack itself instructs direct verification. Two readings: (a) opportunity — operate nightly STR unregulated today; (b) risk — Florence's 2023 text is a ready-made template one council meeting away from adoption next door. Buying property predicated on a regulatory vacuum surviving your hold period is a bet, not a fact.

**The 30+ day exemption — the single most important regulatory fact in the pack.** Stays of 30+ consecutive days are *not* STRs under the ordinance: no permit, no occupancy caps, no TOT exposure, no 30-minute contact, no party enforcement [Data §3]. Residual risk: a definitional amendment (e.g., redefining to 28 days) — possible, low near-term probability, monitor-only. **Confidence: High** that the exemption is explicit in current text; **Medium** that it persists 5+ years.

## 4. Downside scenarios with numbers

Common assumptions (mine): 3BR purchase ~$300K; opex = cleaning (turns × $75), utilities $300/mo, STR insurance $2.2K, maintenance/supplies $2.5K, platform 3%, software/permit/misc $1K; 365-day availability. Levered case: 25% down, 7%/30yr → P&I ≈ $1,576/mo (on $225K) ≈ $18.9K/yr — rates are my assumption, flagged.

| Scenario | Occupancy | ADR | Gross | Opex | **Net** | Unlevered yield | Levered cash flow (25% down) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Bull** — AirDNA world, top-quartile ops | 55% | $149 | ~$30.0K | ~$14.3K | **~$15.7K** | ~5.2% | **≈ −$3.2K/yr** |
| **Base** — Rabbu/AirROI median entrant | 35% | $160 | ~$20.4K | ~$12.5K | **~$7.9K** | ~2.6% | **≈ −$11K/yr** |
| **Bear** — shakeout continues, hotel-anchored pricing | 27% | $135 | ~$13.3K | ~$11.4K | **~$1.9K** | ~0.6% | **≈ −$17K/yr** |

Derivations: gross = occ × ADR × 365 (bull 0.55×149×365 = $29,955; base $20,440; bear $13,298) — cross-checked against provider actuals: bull ≈ AirDNA $27.5K, base ≈ Rabbu $19.1K / AirROI $18.96K, bear ≈ between AirROI bottom-25% ($11.6K) and median ($21.6K) tiers.

**Bear-case add-ons (not in the table above):**
- **Seasonal cash-flow strangulation:** Jan $878/mo and Feb $1,027/mo [Rabbu] against ~$1,000+/mo of fixed opex plus any debt service → deep winter deficits even in the base case.
- **Exit-value risk:** if the current asset prices ($349K avg [Rabbu/Zillow]) carry an STR-fever premium (my assumption — unverifiable from pack, low confidence on magnitude), the bear case removes the marginal buyer and reprices units toward LTR comps: illustration, a 10–15% haircut = **$30–45K loss per property** at exit.
- **Regulatory bear:** Florence tightens (caps/zone restrictions) *and* one neighbor adopts its own ordinance within the hold period → nightly units forced into permit limbo or converted to LTR at the worst possible time — i.e., forced seller into the bear market above.
- **Bear trigger, quantified:** sustained ADR below ~$120 (hotel floor [Data §2]) with occupancy below ~30% → gross <$13K, net ≈ $0 or negative *unlevered*. Two of three providers are already within 5–7 points of that occupancy trigger today.

## 5. What would make me say NO to this proposal

Hard NO triggers — any one is sufficient:

1. **The pro forma is underwritten to AirDNA alone** (55% occ / $27.5K / score 97 as the base case). That's survivorship bias with a price tag. Underwrite to $19–21K gross or don't underwrite.
2. **Leveraged, nightly-STR-only strategy at current asset prices.** Negative carry in *every* scenario (§4) — the model only works as an unlevered, top-quartile-execution bet.
3. **"No regulation" treated as permanent in Sheffield/Tuscumbia/Muscle Shoals** without direct municipal verification [Data §3 — the pack itself says verify].
4. **Construction-crew demand (spec building / Advantage Site) counted in base-case revenue.** No tenant, no timeline [Data §4]. It's option value; underwriting it is fabrication-adjacent.
5. **Day-one top-decile assumptions** ($3,996/mo, 75%+ occ, $319 ADR tier [AirROI]) without a premium property (waterfront/hot-tub class) and a proven operator — 90% of incumbents don't hit those numbers with years of reviews.
6. **No named local operator** for Florence nightly units — the 30-minute contact response requirement [Data §3] is an operational commitment, not paperwork.
7. **Single-channel tourism demand with no healthcare anchor** — heritage tourism alone will not carry winter (Jan–Feb troughs, §1).

## 6. The steelman bull case I could not break

**It is not nightly STR. It is the furnished 30+ day lease — mid-term rental — anchored on healthcare demand, with peak-season nightly optionality.**

**Structure:** acquire 2–3BR units near NAMC/UNA (sub-$300K, below the $349K market average [Rabbu]); lease furnished at $1,800–$2,100/mo, 1–6 month terms, via Furnished Finder + direct + OTA monthly-stay channels; hold the right to flip to nightly STR only in peak months (May–Aug, Oct [Rabbu/AirROI]) if — and only if — the AirDNA world proves real in actuals.

**Why it survives every attack I ran:**

1. **It doesn't use the bear case's inputs.** Its revenue doesn't depend on 31–55% nightly occupancy at all — it depends on contractual monthly leases. The §4 bear table (occ, ADR, RevPAR) simply doesn't apply to it.
2. **Its floor is a known, boring market.** Worst case, an empty mid-term unit reverts to the ordinary rental market (~$800–$1,300/mo for 1BR stock [Data §2]; ~$1,300–1,600/mo for 3BR, my interpolation). That floor is not in a bubble — it's the baseline housing economy.
3. **The furnished premium is observed, not hoped for:** furnished 1BR asking $1,200/mo [Trulia, Data §2] vs unfurnished $750–$975 [Stonebrook] — a $225–$450/mo premium at the *1BR* level; travel-nurse-grade 2–3BR commands more.
4. **It monetizes the only High-confidence demand driver** (§1: healthcare contracts, with Furnished Finder and nurse-group channels already active) and secondarily UNA visiting faculty/families — while keeping tourism upside as a free option rather than a load-bearing wall.
5. **It is the cheapest regulatory posture available:** 30+ day stays are expressly exempt from STR permits, caps, TOT, and the 30-minute-contact regime [Data §3]. No competitor moat is needed when the compliance moat is statutory.
6. **Illustrative economics (my assumptions):** $1,900/mo × 11.5 months = $21.9K gross; opex ≈ $8.9K (utilities incl. ~$3.0K, insurance $1.8K, maintenance $1.5K, furnishing amortization $2.0K, turns/marketing $0.6K) → **net ~$13K ≈ 4.3% unlevered on $300K** — LTR-parity income, with furnished-premium resilience and the nightly-STR call option attached. Note: master-lease arbitrage (rent unfurnished ~$1,300, sublet furnished) only clears ~$250–400/mo/unit after included utilities — thin; **owning beats arbitraging at current prices.**
7. **The competitive set is fragmented and thin** — 4 corporate apartments in Sheffield, independent Studio 6 weekly rates, zero branded extended-stay [Data §2] — while the demand is contractual rather than walk-in.

**What could still kill it (honest residue):** (a) Florence-style ordinance amended to a 28-day STR definition — monitor quarterly; (b) NAMC travel-nurse census cut (medium risk, external assumption); (c) a furnished-supply flood compressing the premium — currently 4 units + OTAs is not a flood. None is evidenced in the pack; all are monitorables, not blockers.

## TL;DR

1. Two of three data providers (Rabbu 31%, AirROI 37.5%) say typical occupancy is ~⅓ and gross is ~$19K/yr — AirDNA's 55%/$27.5K/97-score is a survivorship artifact and must not be the pro forma.
2. Airbnb-channel supply is up +105%/+34.8% while ADR fell 13% — that's a price-war shakeout; a new entrant inherits the median, not the top decile.
3. Base-case nightly STR nets ~$7.9K on a $300K house — *less* than just renting it out long-term (~$12.4K); levered, it loses money in every scenario.
4. Say NO if the plan is AirDNA-underwritten, leveraged, nightly-only, banking on unverified regulatory vacuums, or counting unbuilt spec-building crews as demand.
5. The unbreakable play is the furnished 30+ day lease — statutorily exempt from STR law, anchored on travel-nurse demand, floored by the ordinary rental market, ~4.3% unlevered with peak-season nightly optionality: that version, I sign.

**Assumption register (all mine, not from the pack):** $300K entry price; opex line items as itemized in §2.3/§4; 3BR LTR rent $1,300–1,600/mo interpolated from cited 1BR data; mortgage 25% down at 7%/30yr; furnished 3BR achievable at $1,800–$2,100/mo extrapolated from the observed furnished 1BR ask; national travel-nurse normalization; STR-fever premium embedded in $349K ZHVI (low confidence on magnitude). **Confidence:** High on the shakeout/crossover findings (two-provider agreement); Medium on scenario magnitudes; Low on any single-point forecast in a 94–220-listing market.
